1. Growing Up in Amherst

Born in Amherst, Massachusetts, in 1830, Emily Dickinson spent most of her life in the town where she grew up. She studied at Amherst Academy and later attended Mount Holyoke Female Seminary. She enjoyed reading and spending time in nature.

2. The People Who Shaped Her Life

Emily grew up with her brother Austin and her younger sister Lavinia who later discovered many of Emily's unpublished poems after her death. Her sister's decision to preserve these writings helped introduce Emily's work to readers. Emily also had a close relationship with Susan Gilbert, who became Austin's wife. Although Emily lived a quiet and private life, her letters show that she had strong feelings and meaningful connections with the people around her.

3. Why Death Appeared So Often in Her Poems

Death was one of the subjects Emily Dickinson returned to throughout her writing. During her lifetime, she experienced loss and lived in a society where religion and ideas about the afterlife were important parts of everyday life. Instead of writing about death in only a frightening way, she explored it from different points of view. In Because I could not stop for Death, death is presented as a carriage companion taking the speaker on a journey. Her poems often leave readers wondering about what happens after life and whether it is possible to find answers to such questions.

4. The Way She Saw the World Through Words

Emily Dickinson had a writing style that was different from much of the poetry written during her time. She often used short lines, unusual punctuation, capital letters and rhymes that did not always sound exact. Her use of dashes gave her poems a distinctive rhythm and allowed ideas to remain open to interpretation. She could take something simple, like a bird singing or a walk through the countryside, and turn it into a reflection on hope, freedom or human life.
